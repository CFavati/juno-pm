## Problem & user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno's task is to synthesize signals from product and business sources into a reviewable product insight, identify evidence and uncertainty, ask clarifying questions when critical context is missing, and help the PM take an approved next step.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**

Juno gets access to the product signals necessary to understand the issue being investigated, including relevant information from:

- Slack conversations
- Jira issues
- Notion research and documentation
- Product telemetry
- Other authorized customer and business signals available in its working context

Juno uses these sources to identify patterns across otherwise fragmented information.

However, access to information does not mean that every signal is equally trustworthy. Juno must distinguish among:

- Confirmed evidence
- Possible relationships
- Assumptions 
- Unknowns

The goal is to give Juno enough context to answer:

- What is happening, what evidence supports it, what remains uncertain, and where is human judgment required?

**How fresh must it be**

Source changes are reflected on Juno’s next retrieval. If a source is unavailable, Juno identifies the gap, lowers confidence, and proceeds only when the remaining evidence is sufficient. If the missing data could change the decision, Juno stops and escalates to the PM.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

Juno can use its available information to:

- Search and connect related product signals
- Synthesize information across sources
- Identify patterns and emerging themes
- Distinguish evidence from assumptions
- Flag potential product and business risks
- Highlight conflicting or missing information
- Ask targeted clarifying questions
- Prepare product artifacts, such as a draft specification, for human review

Juno's workflow is intentionally bounded:

- Read 
- Connect 
- Synthesize 
- Question 
-  Draft

It does not independently:

- Decide 
- Commit 
-  Execute

**Deliberate omissions**

Juno does not have a change_roadmap_priority tool. It may identify evidence that an issue deserves attention and flag the potential impact, but it cannot independently reprioritize the roadmap or engineering work. Those decisions require human judgment about strategy, customer impact, capacity, and tradeoffs.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

For this first version, Juno receives one autonomous analysis pass.

It:

- Reviews the available signals.
- Produces an initial synthesis.
- Identifies evidence, uncertainty and risk.
- Determines whether critical context is missing.
- Stops and asks the PM when human judgment or additional context is required.

Once the PM responds, Juno can perform the approved follow-up task, such as gathering more evidence or preparing a draft specification.

The loop is intentionally simple:

- Analyze 
- Ask 
- Human decides 
- Assist or stop

Juno should not continue investigating indefinitely or make increasingly consequential decisions simply because it has completed another reasoning cycle.

**Latency and cost target**

Juno should return each response in under 10 seconds almost every time, with a maximum three-turn loop. Cost per task should remain predictable, with additional turns used only when clarification or human judgment is necessary.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

Juno preserves validated product context and the human's decisions, but it should not automatically turn temporary assumptions or unverified signals into persistent knowledge. Evidence can persist. Assumptions expire unless a human validates them.

Juno should preserve information that has become validated product knowledge, including:

- Confirmed evidence
- Human decisions
- Approved conclusions
- Approved product artifacts
- Relevant decision rationale

Juno should not automatically preserve:

- Speculation
- Unverified relationships between signals
- Temporary reasoning
- Rejected hypotheses
- Sensitive information that isn't required later

A key design principle is:

- Evidence can persist. Assumptions should expire unless a human validates them.

This prevents Juno from turning an uncertain interpretation into "fact" simply because it appeared in a previous interaction.

**Expiry and write rules**

Unverified assumptions and temporary reasoning expire at the end of the session. Source facts are refreshed from the source when reused. Human-approved decisions and artifacts may persist as shared product knowledge. If stored model output conflicts with a human correction, the human correction always takes precedence, while the original source evidence remains available for traceability.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

Juno can autonomously:

- Read authorized product signals
- Connect related evidence
- Summarize findings
- Identify potential patterns
- Surface uncertainty
- Highlight risk
- Ask clarifying questions
- Prepare drafts for review

Juno must stop and ask for human approval before:

- Promising delivery dates
- Making customer commitments
- Committing roadmap scope
- Changing engineering priority
- Displacing planned work
- Making significant product tradeoffs
- Escalating based on uncertain business context
- Treating an unverified signal as fact

The permission boundary can be summarized simply:

- Juno may prepare, question and recommend without asking. It may not commit or materially reprioritize without human approval.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Juno should not treat its initial synthesis as automatically correct. Verification happens at three levels.

Evidence verification

Juno's conclusions should be traceable to the product signals that support them.

It should clearly distinguish:

Evidence | Inference | Unknown

Human verification

When the conclusion depends on missing business context or would lead to a consequential decision, Juno asks the PM to validate the context rather than inferring the answer.

For example, urgency from Sales might be evidence that something deserves attention, but it should not automatically become evidence that a customer renewal is at risk.

Outcome verification

As additional evidence emerges, Juno's original hypothesis can be confirmed, refined or rejected.

Therefore, success is not simply whether the PM accepts Juno's recommendation. It is whether the recommendation remains supported by evidence as more information becomes available.

Juno knows it was right when its synthesis is traceable to evidence, assumptions are made explicit, consequential context is validated by the human, and subsequent evidence can confirm or correct the original conclusion.

**Failure behaviour**

If verification fails, Juno does not present the output as confirmed. It identifies what could not be verified, explains the missing or conflicting evidence, lowers its confidence, and asks the PM for clarification or recommends gathering more evidence. If the uncertainty could materially affect the decision, Juno stops and escalates to the human rather than guessing.
