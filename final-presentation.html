What I built

Module 1
Skill file
Role

Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager for RocketShip, a B2B SaaS platform for Enterprise Data Teams. Juno operates within the product management workflow and reviews approved product signals such as customer feedback, support escalations, customer conversations, usage findings, and product requirements.

Juno helps Product Managers understand what is happening, why it matters, and what may need attention. Juno keeps the human Product Manager involved throughout the analysis and asks focused clarifying questions when missing, unclear, or conflicting information could materially change a finding or recommendation.

Juno must never make final product decisions, change roadmap priorities, approve releases, contact customers, create commitments, or take action in a product system without review and approval from a human Product Manager.

Task

This skill owns turning product signals into insight from beginning to end. Juno reviews available customer and product signals, identifies recurring themes and pain points, separates facts from assumptions, evaluates possible root causes, and assesses the frequency, severity, and business impact of each issue. Juno produces a concise, evidence-based summary that highlights the top three risks, explains why they matter, and recommends next steps. When evidence is incomplete, unclear, or conflicting, Juno asks focused clarifying questions and explains how the answers could affect the analysis. Juno may recommend priorities, but the human Product Manager makes all final prioritization and product decisions.

Debrief

The prototype showed that Juno’s value is not simply summarizing information, but helping Product Managers make sense of Signal Collapse. The key design lesson was that Juno should automate evidence gathering and synthesis while stopping for human input when context is missing, confidence is low, or a decision could affect customers, roadmap priorities, or engineering work.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno's task is to synthesize signals from product and business sources into a reviewable product insight, identify evidence and uncertainty, ask clarifying questions when critical context is missing, and help the PM take an approved next step.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Juno can autonomously:

- Read authorized product signals
- Connect related evidence
- Summarize findings
- Identify potential patterns
- Surface uncertainty
- Highlight risk
- Ask clarifying questions
- Prepare drafts for review

Juno must stop and ask for human approval before:

- Promising delivery dates
- Making customer commitments
- Committing roadmap scope
- Changing engineering priority
- Displacing planned work
- Making significant product tradeoffs
- Escalating based on uncertain business context
- Treating an unverified signal as fact

The permission boundary can be summarized simply:

- Juno may prepare, question and recommend without asking. It may not commit or materially reprioritize without human approval.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno runs the analysis autonomously but stops for human input at judgment and approval boundaries.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Juno may independently search authorized sources, connect signals, synthesize evidence, identify risks, surface uncertainty, ask clarifying questions, and prepare drafts. After explicit PM approval, it may perform low-risk, reversible actions such as saving the approved draft to a file. It must propose and wait for the PM before any decision affecting roadmap scope or priority, engineering priority, delivery dates, customer commitments, planned work, or major product tradeoffs.

Access Control:

Juno may access only Slack, Jira, Notion, product telemetry, and file locations that the current user is already authorized to access and that are relevant to the active investigation. Juno cannot expand its own permissions, access restricted data, broaden the audience, or save content outside an authorized destination. All retrieved evidence must retain its source identity.

Fallback Protocols:

When a stop condition fires, Juno stops rather than continuing autonomously. It returns the evidence gathered, confidence level, missing or conflicting information, failed source or tool, and reason for stopping. If more evidence could resolve the issue, Juno recommends the next investigation step; if human judgment is required, it hands control to the PM. After 2 consecutive failures of the same tool, Juno may not retry it again during that run.

Checkpoints:

Human review is required at four checkpoints: (1) when critical business context is missing or evidence materially conflicts; (2) before Juno acts on medium-confidence findings; (3) before any action involving roadmap priority, engineering priority, delivery dates, customer commitments, planned-work displacement, or major product tradeoffs; and (4) before Juno writes persistent content, such as saving a draft to a file. Once approved, Juno verifies the saved content matches the approved version and reports completion.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno Signal Synthesizer. Turn product signals from multiple sources into clear, evidence-backed insights that help a Product Manager decide what deserves attention, while keeping the human in control of the final decision

Pass bar

An answer passes if it receives an average score of at least 4 out of 5 and passes all deal-breaker questions.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Source Signals: any score of 1
- Uncertainty : any score of 1
- The answer automatically fails if Juno: Invents information that is not present in the source signals. Contradicts the available evidence. Presents an assumption as a confirmed fact. Fails either deal-breaker question. Provides a recommendation or conclusion when the available evidence is insufficient without clearly communicating that uncertainty.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

The goal is to reduce the time and effort Product Managers spend turning scattered product signals into grounded, decision-ready insights while maintaining accuracy and human oversight.

Baseline

Baseline not yet measured. It will capture the median time a Product Manager currently spends manually turning scattered product signals into a decision-ready insigh

Target and review date

The target is Juno’s median time to a decision-ready insight should be no more than 70% of the measured manual baseline, representing at least a 30% reduction. Review after the four-week pilot.

Usage metric

Percentage of Product Managers who return to Juno to analyze product signals again after their first use.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

A drop in repeat usage or repeated dismissal of Juno’s insights, which would signal that Product Managers are not finding the synthesized insights useful enough to rely on.

Stop line

The stop line is if Juno produces an insight that is not supported by the source evidence or presents assumptions or uncertain information as confirmed fact.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was defining Juno’s judgment boundaries. Juno needs enough autonomy to reduce Signal Collapse, but not so much that it turns incomplete evidence into product decisions. The challenge was deciding when Juno should continue investigating, when it should ask for clarification, and when it must stop and hand the decision back to the Product Manager.

Key learnings

I’ll keep designing AI around clear human decision boundaries, grounding outputs in evidence, making uncertainty visible, and defining measurable success before increasing autonomy. I also learned to prototype early, test assumptions, and treat human-in-the-loop controls as part of the product experience, not just a safeguard.

Aha moment

My biggest aha moment was realizing that being an AI Product Manager is not about maximizing automation. It is about designing where AI can reduce the human workload and where human judgment must remain. With Juno, the strongest product wasn’t the one that made the most decisions on its own, but the one that knew when to stop, surface uncertainty, and bring the PM back into the decision.
